## Problem
Agent OS should work for anyone building legitimate software. Some of those
people are not technical, and not every project is enterprise software. Today,
adopting the template is a manual, technical checklist (README.md, "Adopting it
in a project", *observed*). Nothing records whose problem the team is solving,
what a good result looks like, or what data is at stake.

Evidence: the stakeholder's review of the first draft of this spec (2026-10-11,
relayed by the coordinating agent): "Keep it generic enough and not assume that
someone's just building enterprise software... we should be adaptable", and
"Don't we want this to be agile as you're continuing?". Their example user is a
school professional who writes many reports about children and handles highly
sensitive personal data. That person could not have answered the first draft's
questions ("how are dependencies installed and tests run", "will it handle
personal data, payments, health data?").

Who has it:
- **The person with the problem**, who may be non-technical.
- **The stakeholder**, who sets the budget and the risk limits and merges the
  rules. This may be the same person.

## Goal
`/onboard` is a short kickoff, not a requirements document. In plain language,
it learns what the person needs and what data is at stake. It ends with a living
intake, a short backlog, and one small first slice for the team to build next.
Nobody has to answer a technical question.

## How it works (what, not how)

### Three parts, each answered by the right person
| Part | Who answers | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Discovery** | The person with the problem, in plain language | Their work, what takes the most time, what a good outcome looks like, who uses the result, and a made-up or de-identified example of today's output |
| **Setup** | The agents propose it; the person only confirms a plain-English summary | How it could be built: maybe a document workflow, a website, or an existing or no-code tool instead of new code (build or reuse). Plus where it runs and how it is checked. Inferred from the repo, or from sensible defaults for the problem. Each proposal is labelled *observed* or *inferred*. |
| **Guardrails** | The stakeholder | Budget, and what must never happen. Autonomy starts at level 1 and is not asked. |

The sensitive-data question sits between Discovery and Guardrails. Either
person can answer it.

### The questions (hard cap: 8, plus one yes/no confirmation of the Setup summary)
| # | Part | Question (each with an example and a "don't know" option) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | Discovery | What is your work, and who do you do it for? |
| Q2 | Discovery | What takes up most of your time? |
| Q3 | Discovery | If this worked, what would be different? How would you know? |
| Q4 | Discovery | Who reads or uses what you produce? |
| Q5 | Discovery | Show an example of what you produce today. Use a made-up one, or one with every real detail removed. |
| Q6 | Data | Will it include any of: names, birthdates, ID numbers, diagnoses, test scores, disability or special-education details, children, health, family, school or payment details? |
| Q7 | Guardrails | What may this cost each month, including anything you already pay for? |
| Q8 | Guardrails | What must never happen? (for example, a report going to the wrong family) |

**Real sensitive data never goes into the interview, the intake or an
example.** The skill says this before Q1, and again before Q5 and Q6, in those
words or plainer ones.

### Sensitive data
- The `security-compliance` agent maps the Q6 answers to data categories. It
  flags rules that *might* apply, for example United States student-records or
  special-education law for a school (*inferred*, not assessed). It recommends
  that the person check with their organisation: employer, school district or
  compliance officer. It never says "compliant".
- **Highly sensitive data stops the build.** If the answers include highly
  sensitive data, onboarding raises an `/escalate` decision before anything is
  built. Highly sensitive means the list in "Decided on approval", item 3. The
  decision covers whether, and how, the product may handle that data at all,
  including whether it may pass through an AI model.

### Agile: what onboarding produces
- **`docs/project/intake.md`**: the answers, the Setup summary, the data
  flags and the autonomy level. It is a living document: the team updates it as
  it learns. Re-running `/onboard`, or editing the file, updates it. This is a
  protected path.
- **`docs/project/backlog.md`**: a short list of small slices, each one plain
  sentence with a "done when". Exactly one is marked **next**. Each slice then gets its own short spec from the product manager
  ("Decided on approval", item 5).
- **`docs/project/team.md`**: a diagram of this project's people, team and
  flow, with a text version that says the same thing. Accessibility
  requirement: every visual has an equivalent text version, because some
  people cannot form or hold mental images. *Assumption:* a Mermaid block, because GitHub renders it.
- **`CLAUDE.md`**: a short project section between fixed markers that points
  agents to the intake and states the autonomy level. It is a protected path.

Because the intake and `CLAUDE.md` are protected, the stakeholder merges the
onboarding PR.

### Autonomy levels
| Level | Meaning | How a project gets it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The stakeholder merges every PR. The CI principal engineer still reviews each one, but its Approve never turns on auto-merge. | Every new project starts here. |
| 2 | Routine PRs auto-merge when the CI principal engineer approves and every required check is green. PRs that change protected files are still the stakeholder's. This is agent-os today (`docs/decisions/2026-10-11-merge-authority.md`, option D). | Earned by evidence (proposed: 5 merged PRs needing no follow-up fix). The stakeholder makes the switch by editing the intake in a PR they merge. |

### Borrowed from AIS-OS (MIT, copyright 2026 Nate Herk), and what differs
Read from a local clone on 2026-10-11 (*observed*).
- **Borrowed, with credit:**
  - an intake file as the single source of truth;
  - one question at a time, each answer saved straight away;
  - a hard question cap;
  - re-runnable, skipping answered questions;
  - read the existing `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` first, and pre-fill only
    what the repo supports, citing it;
  - push back on vague outcomes;
  - never ask for API keys;
  - a short closing screen with one next step.
- **Differs:**
  - Ours sets up a team that builds something for someone. AIS-OS sets up one
    person's assistant.
  - Ours splits the questions by who can answer them.
  - Ours asks concretely about sensitive data, and stops the build for highly
    sensitive data.
  - Ours ends in a backlog and a first slice.
  - Ours goes through a PR, because it writes protected files.
  - Ours uses none of AIS-OS's trademarked framework names, its framework
    vocabulary or its closing prompt.

## Acceptance criteria
The conversation itself is checked through the skill's file (AC-1 to AC-4).
Everything else is a deterministic, tested script. Where those scripts live is
the engineering manager's decision.

### The skill and the questions
- [ ] AC-1: Given `.claude/skills/onboard/SKILL.md`, then:
  - its frontmatter has `name: onboard` and a `description`;
  - it names Sonnet as its model;
  - it states the cap of 8 questions plus one confirmation;
  - it refers to no path outside the repository (lesson L-0005);
  - it credits AIS-OS with a link and the MIT license;
  - it contains none of "Three Ms", "Default Shift" or "Mindset framework".
- [ ] AC-2: Given the intake template, then:
  - it has at most 8 questions, each with an example and a "don't know" option;
  - every question is at most 25 words;
  - no question contains any of these words: dependency, stack, repository,
    CI, deploy, test command, API, database, server, PII, compliance,
    personal data. Matching is case-insensitive and whole-word, so "CI" does
    not match "special".
- [ ] AC-3: Given the intake template and the skill file, then:
  - Q6 names names, birthdates, ID numbers, diagnoses, test scores, disability
    or special-education details, children, health, family, school and payment
    details;
  - the warning never to use real sensitive data appears before Q1, before Q5
    and before Q6.
- [ ] AC-4: Given the skill file, then it tells the agent to:
  - ask one question at a time and save each answer before asking the next;
  - never ask the person a Setup question, and instead show a plain-English
    Setup summary to confirm;
  - consider existing or no-code tools;
  - never say "compliant", never ask for secrets, never fetch web content,
    and never merge.

### The intake: resumable, idempotent, protected
- [ ] AC-5: Given an intake with Q1-Q3 answered, when the status command runs,
  then it lists Q4-Q8 and the Setup confirmation as still open, grouped by
  part. Answers of "don't know" are listed separately, as follow-ups.
- [ ] AC-6: Given an answer that is already recorded, when it is recorded again,
  then the file is byte-identical. When a different answer is recorded, only
  that question's section changes. An intake saved with CRLF line endings gives
  the same results as the LF version.
- [ ] AC-7: Given the autonomy level:
  - a newly created intake is at level 1;
  - the recording script refuses to set the level;
  - a level of 2 set by hand survives a re-run;
  - validation fails for any level other than 1 or 2.
- [ ] AC-8: Given `findProtected` in `tools/review/protected-paths.mjs`, then
  `docs/project/intake.md` is protected, and `docs/project/backlog.md` and
  `docs/project/team.md` are not.

### Sensitive data
- [ ] AC-9: Given Q6 answers that include a highly sensitive item, when the
  status command runs, then it reports `BLOCKED: awaiting decision` with the
  decision file's path. No backlog slice can be marked ready until that file
  in `docs/decisions/` has `status: decided`.
- [ ] AC-16: Given each highly sensitive category in "Decided on approval"
  item 3, when Q6 is answered with only the matching option, then the status
  command reports `BLOCKED: awaiting decision`. Every category maps to at least
  one Q6 option, checked by a test.
- [ ] AC-10: Given intake text that looks like a real identifier (an email
  address, a phone number, a 9-digit ID number, or a full date), when it is
  validated, then it warns, names the question, and asks the person to confirm
  the data is made up. This is a safety net, not a guarantee.

### What onboarding produces
- [ ] AC-11: Given a complete intake, when files are generated, then:
  - only the intake, the backlog, the diagram file and the `CLAUDE.md` project
    section change;
  - outside its markers, `CLAUDE.md` is byte-identical;
  - the project section is at most 100 words;
  - generating a second time changes nothing.
- [ ] AC-12: Given a generated backlog, then:
  - it has 3 to 7 slices;
  - each slice has one plain sentence and a "done when";
  - exactly one slice is marked next.
- [ ] AC-13: Given a generated diagram file, then it has a Mermaid block and a
  text version. Both name the person with the problem, the stakeholder, every
  role in `.claude/agents/`, and the autonomy level.

### Level 1 in CI (Decision 2)
- [ ] AC-14: Given the auto-merge decision, with three inputs (the verdict,
  whether protected files changed, and the level read from `main`'s intake), then:
  - approve, unprotected, level 1: no auto-merge, the status is still
    `success`, and the comment says the stakeholder merges;
  - approve, unprotected, level 2: auto-merge on the reviewed commit, as today;
  - any other verdict, or protected files changed: no auto-merge;
  - intake missing or invalid on `main`: treated as level 1, and the comment
    says why;
  - a PR that changes the level: judged by the level on `main`, not the PR's.
- [ ] AC-15: Given the onboarding scripts, when they are checked statically, then
  they import no network module, and their tests pass with the network
  unavailable.

## Non-functional requirements
- **Security:**
  - The level-1 workflow change touches a protected file and merge authority.
    It needs a decision record in `docs/decisions/` amending the
    merge-authority decision, set to `decided` by the stakeholder, and
    `security-compliance` review, before it merges.
  - The autonomy level fails closed, is read only from `main`, and only the
    stakeholder can change it.
  - No secrets are asked for or stored, and gitleaks scans the intake.
  - The onboarding session does no web research (lesson L-0008).
- **Cost:**
  - The interview runs on Sonnet. Generating files and deciding auto-merge use
    no model tokens.
  - At most 8 questions, in one session or several short ones.
  - The team may recommend existing or free tools when they serve the person
    better than new code.
- **Performance:** the interview takes about 15 minutes in total (*assumption*);
  generating files takes under 5 seconds.
- **Accessibility:**
  - No jargon, and every question is at most 25 words (AC-2).
  - The Setup summary is in plain English.
  - Every diagram has a text version (AC-13).
  - The closing screen is at most 5 lines: what was written, the PR to review,
    and the next slice.
- **Privacy and data:**
  - Never paste real sensitive data, with the warnings in AC-3 and the
    detector in AC-10.
  - The intake is committed, so the skill warns that it may be public if the
    repo is.
  - Highly sensitive data stops the build until the stakeholder decides
    (AC-9).
  - The skill recommends checking with the person's organisation, and never
    claims compliance.
- **Portability:** Windows, macOS and cloud sessions. Repo files only (lesson
  L-0005). Node 20+, no new dependencies.

## Delivery
*Assumption:* about three small PRs, all merged by the stakeholder because they
touch protected paths:
1. The skill, the intake template, the record, status and validate scripts,
   and the protected path (AC-1 to AC-10, AC-16).
2. Generating the backlog, the diagram and the `CLAUDE.md` section (AC-11 to
   AC-13, AC-15).
3. The level-1 rule in `principal-review`, after its decision record is
   decided (AC-14).

## Out of scope (v2 or later)
- Generating the CI `tests` job from the Setup answers, and the separate job
  for the template's tool tests (Decision 5).
- Importing other projects' lessons (Decision 3).
- Adding rows to `docs/landscape.md`.
- Writing each slice's spec automatically.
- Autonomy levels above 2; measuring the evidence for level 2, or switching
  levels automatically.
- Setup actions for specific runtimes; monorepos; a Codex mirror; a
  Windows/macOS CI matrix.
- Changing the project's application code.

## Decisions
Decided by the stakeholder on 2026-10-11 ("go with your recommendation"),
relayed by the coordinating agent. The stakeholder confirms them by approving
this spec.
1. **Autonomy levels.** Two in v1: level 1, where the stakeholder merges every
   PR, and level 2, where routine PRs auto-merge on the CI principal engineer's
   approval. New projects start at level 1. Level 2 is earned by evidence
   (proposed: 5 merged PRs needing no follow-up fix), and the stakeholder makes
   the switch. Applies in v1.
2. **No auto-merge at level 1.** `principal-review` reads the level and skips
   auto-merge at level 1. This needs a decision record and security review.
   Applies in v1 (AC-14).
3. **Imported lessons** arrive as `status: proposed`. Deferred to v2 with
   lesson import.
4. **The intake** is at `docs/project/intake.md`, a protected path. Applies in
   v1 (AC-8).
5. **The template's tool tests** run in their own CI job, separate from the
   project's `tests` job. Deferred to v2 with generating the CI tests job.
6. **The interview runs on Sonnet.** Applies in v1 (AC-1).

## Decided on approval (stakeholder, 2026-10-11)
The stakeholder approved the spec "for now" and took the recommendation on
each open question:
1. **agent-os gets its own intake at level 2** in the PR that makes the
   workflow read autonomy levels, so today's auto-merge keeps working.
2. **A follow-up fix** is a later PR that reverts or fixes something an earlier
   PR introduced. 5 merged PRs without one is guidance, not an automatic rule;
   the stakeholder decides the switch to level 2.
3. **Highly sensitive** (raises `/escalate` before anything is built):
   information about children; diagnoses or test scores; disabilities or
   special education; health, including mental health; payment details;
   government ID numbers.
4. **When the person with the problem is not the stakeholder**, the
   stakeholder runs Discovery with them and records the answers. They do not
   need Claude Code. A shareable form is out of scope for v1.
5. **Onboarding names the next slice only.** The product manager writes that
   slice's spec as the following step.

"For now" means this spec may be revised as discovery with real people teaches
us more; a revision goes back to the stakeholder.
